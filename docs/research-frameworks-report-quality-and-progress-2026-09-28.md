# How research systems build good reports and measure progress

**Research date:** 28 September 2026

**Purpose:** compare public research systems and evaluation methods with Scout's current workflow, then identify a small, testable way to measure progress toward a useful, supported report.

**Method:** read the authors' papers, official documentation, and published code. This is a design review, not a head-to-head experiment. No external system was run, no model/provider calls were made, and vendor or project benchmark claims were not independently replicated. Repository code links describe the public version reviewed on this date; the LangChain repository is archived, while other repositories may change.

## Executive assessment

The systems that generate convincing reports do more than retrieve many pages. They **form an explicit coverage target**, search for evidence under several perspectives or subtasks, preserve usable evidence through compression, synthesize around the reader's question, and separately check whether the output's claims are actually supported. The most important lesson for Scout is to make progress observable at *three different boundaries*: what the run did, what evidence it established, and what the reader can verify in the final answer. Search count, citations count, or a model's “done” signal is only activity unless tied to those boundaries.

None of the surveyed systems publishes a trustworthy universal “percentage researched.” The closest approaches are stage-specific: STORM tests outline coverage against a reference; Magentic-One's progress ledger self-reflects and detects stalls; GPT Researcher exposes tree/iteration counters; Open Deep Research lets a supervisor decide when to finish under iteration limits; OpenScholar revises a draft against retrieval and citation feedback. Their *evaluations* then inspect the finished work with independent rubrics, citation-support judgments, or experts. These signals answer different questions and should remain separate. [STORM paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.347.pdf), [Magentic-One documentation](https://microsoft.github.io/autogen/dev/user-guide/agentchat-user-guide/magentic-one.html), [GPT Researcher progress documentation](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/docs/docs/gpt-researcher/gptr/deep_research.md), [Open Deep Research code](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research/blob/main/src/open_deep_research/deep_researcher.py), [OpenScholar paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10072-4).

For Scout, the near-term opportunity is a **versioned, code-owned progress snapshot** based on the existing plan, ledger, tool outcomes, checks, and report. It would show each material requirement as searched, evidenced, still uncertain, addressed in the answer, or explicitly unresolved, while keeping operational progress and post-run quality scores in separate fields. It must never infer that a claim is true because a quote string matched, that a gap is closed because a deep dive was assigned to it, or that the answer is supported because a hidden claim inventory passed. Those are current audit risks, not desirable progress rules. [Independent audit](architectural-audit-2026-09-28-independent.md), [workflow addendum](workflow-audit-addendum-2026-09-28.md).

## A common model: four boundaries

| Boundary | Question | Useful signals | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plan** | What must this report answer? | Explicit requirements, perspectives, decision criteria, source constraints, intended reader. | Number of research questions alone. |
| **Evidence** | What has actually been established? | Distinct relevant works, accessible passages, source identity, claim-to-passage support, contradictions, important omissions. | Page count, quote substring match, or citations printed. |
| **Delivery** | Did the visible report answer it faithfully? | Requirement-to-answer mapping, sentence-to-claim/source support, clear caveats, organization, directness. | A clean hidden claim list or a polished summary. |
| **Operation** | Did the run finish within bounds? | Stage state, elapsed time, spent/reserved dollars, retries, cutoffs, unavailable sources, stop reason. | `complete` as a quality verdict. |

This partition is an inference from the surveyed systems and benchmarks, not a standard those projects all implement. The distinction is essential because a retrieval improvement may increase evidence without improving writing; a writer may improve presentation while inventing unsupported detail; and a run may exhaust its budget after producing a useful partial answer. [DeepResearch Bench methods](https://deepresearch-bench.github.io/static/papers/deepresearch-bench.pdf), [DeepResearch Bench II](https://github.com/imlrz/DeepResearch-Bench-II), [Scout evaluation design](evaluation.md).

## Systems examined

### STORM and Co-STORM: make the outline a research artifact

**Report path.** STORM identifies perspectives on a topic, has simulated writers ask source-grounded experts follow-up questions, collects references, refines an outline, then writes a cited article from the outline and references. Co-STORM adds an interactive discourse and dynamic mind map so a person can steer the exploration. This is optimized for broad, Wikipedia-like exposition, not a short decision answer. [Project description](https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm/blob/main/README.md), [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.347.pdf).

**Progress and evaluation.** The authors treat the outline as a measurable intermediate artifact: heading soft recall and heading entity recall compare it with recent human-written articles in FreshWiki. They assess final articles using lexical/entity measures, rubric judgments of interest, organization, relevance and coverage, model-judged citation recall/precision, and experienced Wikipedia editors. In their editor study, the proportion rated organized rose 25 percentage points and the proportion rated broad in coverage rose 10 points against an outline-driven RAG baseline; verifiability did **not** improve in that table. The paper explicitly finds unsupported inferential links despite citations. These are study results for its task distribution, not a general proof that an outline makes every report sound. [Evaluation and human-study tables](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.347.pdf).

**What transfers to Scout.** An explicit coverage structure is valuable before synthesis, and its quality can be inspected separately from writing. Scout already has a `ResearchPlan` and coverage items, so it does not need STORM's simulated conversations or a new mind-map service. A plan quality check should ask whether material aspects and perspectives are represented; the existing planner validator checks structure/IDs, not semantic completeness. An outline or section map could be a *projection* of current requirements for broad reports, tested on stored runs before adding another generation step. [Scout plan schema](../src/research_loop/schemas.py), [planner validation](../src/research_loop/agents.py).

### LangChain Open Deep Research: supervisor, compressed notes, final writer

**Report path.** The archived implementation clarifies a user request, writes a research brief, lets a supervisor delegate parallel researcher tasks, compresses each researcher's tool/AI transcript into findings, then prompts a final report model with accumulated notes. The supervisor can call `ResearchComplete`; the graph also stops when it has no tool calls or exceeds the configured research-iteration limit. Researchers likewise stop on a completion call, no tool call, or a tool-call limit. [Graph and supervisor code](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research/blob/main/src/open_deep_research/deep_researcher.py), [state definitions](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research/blob/main/src/open_deep_research/state.py).

**Progress and evaluation.** Research iterations, tool calls, notes, and completion calls are observable *process* state. `ResearchComplete` is a model decision, not a verified coverage calculation. Its README describes evaluation through DeepResearch Bench and reports RACE scores and resource use for several configurations; RACE judges the **final report**, outside the research graph. Compression is the critical handoff: the writer sees compressed findings, so any decisive caveat or citation lost there cannot be recovered from its report prompt. This is a source-code inference from the state and `final_report_generation` path, not a measured loss rate. [Source code](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research/blob/main/src/open_deep_research/deep_researcher.py), [evaluation README](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research/blob/main/README.md).

**What transfers to Scout.** Scout has a stronger code-owned evidence boundary: its synthesizer sees the checked ledger rather than arbitrary compressed researcher prose. Its `diagnose` projections already separate facts seen in research, stated in claims, and delivered in the report. Extend that stage attribution to the *reader-visible answer* before copying a supervisor loop. The useful borrowed idea is to record why research stopped, including a model-declared sufficiency versus a deadline or cap. [Scout evidence ledger](../src/research_loop/evidence.py), [diagnosis design](evaluation.md#where-the-points-were-lost-research-diagnose).

### GPT Researcher: branch counters are useful telemetry, not evidence quality

**Report path.** The core system has a planner generate subquestions, gathers and summarizes resources for each, then filters/aggregates them into a report. Its deep mode recursively explores configurable breadth and depth and produces a cited report. The publisher and section-oriented deep-agent example show a more elaborate editing path, but these are different configurations of the project; they should not be treated as one fixed pipeline. [Core README](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/README.md), [deep-mode documentation](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/docs/docs/gpt-researcher/gptr/deep_research.md), [deep-agent example](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/deep_agents/README.md).

**Progress and evaluation.** Deep mode's callback exposes current/total depth and breadth, current query, and completed/total queries. These tell a user how much *scheduled work* has run; failed branches may be skipped, and a completed query need not add useful evidence. The project also ships SimpleQA-style accuracy/F1, cost, latency, and source-coverage evaluations, a source-versus-report hallucination check, and a published 10-task comparison using DeepResearch Bench RACE and FACT. That comparison reports many more verified citations from its research tools than from raw search, but only a small report-quality gain and citation precision of roughly 56% in the cited setup. It is a project-run, small-sample result. Citation *abundance* and citation *precision* need separate columns. [Progress documentation](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/docs/docs/gpt-researcher/gptr/deep_research.md), [evaluation README](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/evals/README.md), [benchmark protocol and limits](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/deep_agents/BENCHMARK.md).

**What transfers to Scout.** Expose stage, completed scout/deep-dive count, current cost/time, and query/read outcomes as honest operational telemetry. Do not label breadth/depth counters “coverage.” For effectiveness, count **new, relevant, checked evidence per requirement**, not queries. Because Scout's recent audit found duplicate URL source IDs, normalize identities before using “distinct sources” as a progress measure. [Workflow addendum W04](workflow-audit-addendum-2026-09-28.md#w04--p2-one-document-can-become-multiple-numbered-sources).

### OpenScholar: retrieval, draft feedback, and citation check

**Report path.** OpenScholar specializes in scientific literature: a domain data store, retrievers/rerankers, a citation-backed draft, self-feedback that can request more retrieval, and a final citation-verification pass. Its paper caps feedback at three items for efficient inference. Ablations report lower correctness and/or citation accuracy when reranking, feedback, or citation verification is removed; the largest loss in the described ablation came from removing reranking. It also shows that simply adding more retrieved passages can harm a smaller model after a point. [Nature paper, method and ablations](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10072-4).

**Progress and evaluation.** The loop's progress is whether feedback items have been addressed and whether new passages help the draft; that is more content-oriented than an iteration count, but the feedback and citation decisions are still model judgments. ScholarQABench combines automatic citation/correctness measures with expert assessment of coverage, coherence, writing quality, and factual correctness on scientific long-form answers. The reported superiority is on the paper's corpus and questions, not an across-domain warranty. [Methods and evaluation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10072-4).

**What transfers to Scout.** Targeted post-draft checking is promising only after the visible answer has a sentence-to-evidence map. Scout's current support audit checks its model-written claim inventory and verified quotes, not the full displayed answer and summary; a feedback loop based on that inventory could polish the wrong object. First measure what the reader sees. A bounded, deterministic gap decision can then select one needed follow-up, within existing workflow roles and dollar/time caps. [Independent audit A02/A04](architectural-audit-2026-09-28-independent.md), [Scout support audit](../src/research_loop/audit.py).

### Anthropic Research: dynamic delegation and a separate citation pass

**Report path.** Anthropic describes a lead researcher that plans, delegates parallel subagents, examines returned findings, and decides whether more research is needed. It then passes findings and the draft to a CitationAgent that locates specific citations. This is a public engineering description, not inspectable source code. It reports that vague subagent assignments created duplicate work and missed aspects, and that agents sometimes kept searching after sufficient results. [Engineering account](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system).

**Progress and evaluation.** The lead's “sufficient information” judgment is dynamic but not an external validity check. Anthropic says it evaluates factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality, and tool efficiency with rubric judging and human testing; it warns that agents can take different valid paths, so a prescribed trajectory is a poor universal target. Its reported gains and evaluation alignment are internal, so they should be treated as design evidence rather than directly comparable benchmark results. [Evaluation section](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system).

**What transfers to Scout.** Keep tasks specific enough to reduce duplicate searches and gaps; judge outcomes by answer/evidence quality and process cost rather than requiring the same query sequence. Scout should not add a CitationAgent as a new run role without first proving that sentence-to-source verification of its existing output fails in a way a simpler code check cannot catch; repository policy also requires measurement and approval for new roles. A separate *post-run* citation audit remains compatible with that boundary. [Repository instructions](../AGENTS.md), [Scout evaluation design](evaluation.md).

### Magentic-One: an explicit task/progress ledger with a weak semantic guarantee

Magentic-One is a general web/file task agent rather than a dedicated report writer. Its orchestrator maintains a Task Ledger (facts and plan) and repeatedly updates a Progress Ledger to ask if the task is complete; when stalled, it replans. This makes completion and stall reasoning explicit, a useful operational pattern. But self-reflection alone cannot establish that a cited scientific or policy claim follows from a source. Its value for Scout is the *separation between recorded task state and recorded progress*, not a new orchestrator role or a percentage bar. [Microsoft AutoGen documentation](https://microsoft.github.io/autogen/dev/user-guide/agentchat-user-guide/magentic-one.html).

## How the main evaluation methods measure report quality

| Method | Object judged | Strength | Important limit for Scout |
|---|---|---|---|
| **STORM/FreshWiki** | Outline headings against recent articles; final article readability, coverage and citation entailment; editor ratings. | Exposes a pre-writing signal and checks final presentation. | A Wikipedia outline is an imperfect target for direct answers or disputed decisions; lexical/entity recall can reward irrelevant breadth. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.347.pdf). |
| **DeepResearch Bench RACE** | Final report against a reference on comprehensiveness, insight/depth, instruction following and readability, with task-specific weights. | Tests reader-facing report quality beyond fact inclusion. | Reference and judge choice can bias a score; it does not itself prove every citation. [Paper](https://deepresearch-bench.github.io/static/papers/deepresearch-bench.pdf). |
| **DeepResearch Bench FACT** | Deduplicated statement–URL pairs; fetches pages and judges support. Reports citation accuracy and effective supported citations per task. | Separates reliability from abundance; inspects cited *statements*. | Page retrieval at scoring time can fail/change; judging pair support still needs calibration. [Paper](https://deepresearch-bench.github.io/static/papers/deepresearch-bench.pdf). |
| **DeepResearch Bench II** | Atomic, expert-derived rubrics for information recall, analysis and presentation, with per-item evidence from the submitted report. | Reveals which kind of expert-report content was missed; useful stage diagnostic. | A rubric measures agreement with one expert reference and its task framing; a high score alone is no citation-support verdict. [Maintainer README](https://github.com/imlrz/DeepResearch-Bench-II). |
| **ScholarQABench** | Scientific long-form answers: coverage, coherence, writing, factual correctness and citations, with expert review. | Directly tests literature synthesis and cites source material. | Its corpus/task distribution does not represent broad web research. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10072-4). |

The recurring pattern is **multi-axis measurement**. A single aggregate score hides whether a change retrieved more facts, made better inferences, improved writing, or cited them correctly. Scout's current `research grade`, `assess`, `audit`, and `diagnose` already aim at these different questions. Its audit found a mismatch between the claim inventory those tools inspect and the text the reader actually sees, so making one versioned reader artifact is the prerequisite for comparing those judgments. [Scout evaluation page](evaluation.md), [independent audit](architectural-audit-2026-09-28-independent.md).

## A progress contract for Scout

### 1. Record events, then derive a snapshot

Use existing `ResearchPlan`, `EvidenceLedger`, call/tool outcomes, `RunChecks`, and `FinalReport` to produce a **versioned snapshot after each workflow stage**. A row for each material requirement should contain: stable requirement ID and wording; responsible scout(s); searches/reads attempted; canonical source IDs and access levels; candidate claim IDs; code-checked quote/identity marks; any separate semantic-support verdict; contradiction/unresolved reason; and the exact visible answer sentence or section that addresses it. Store the snapshot with the run, and render a compact human-readable summary. Dynamic open items discovered by research should be added with their origin and not silently change the original denominator. This is a proposed design, not current behavior. [Current schemas](../src/research_loop/schemas.py), [ledger coverage implementation](../src/research_loop/evidence.py), [renderer](../src/research_loop/render.py).

### 2. Give each requirement an honest state

A useful sequence is `planned → searched → evidence_candidate → supported_for_report → addressed`, plus terminal `unresolved` and `not_applicable`. Movement must be monotone only when the underlying evidence survives checks. `evidence_candidate` means a source was found, not that it entails a claim. `supported_for_report` needs a claim-to-passage support judgment and an appropriate source, not merely a matching quote. `addressed` means the *visible* answer either states a supported conclusion or explicitly explains the uncertainty. Conflicts should remain visible; two opposing sources do not cancel into “done.” This design draws on FACT's statement–URL check and STORM's separate outline/article evaluations. [FACT](https://deepresearch-bench.github.io/static/papers/deepresearch-bench.pdf), [STORM](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.347.pdf).

### 3. Show a small vector, not a single percentage

For a run, display: **workflow stage and stop reason**; **requirements addressed / unresolved / untouched**; **decisive claims with verified support / needing review**; **unique accessible source works**, split by full text, abstract and snippet; **cost/time used and remaining**; and **report checks** such as uncited reader-visible sentences. These are interpretable counts with denominators and provenance, not interchangeable scores. “5 of 6 requirements addressed” should say whether the sixth was contradicted, not found, or cut off. Do not use `complete` as a synonym for “correct.” [Scout current checks](../src/research_loop/scout.py), [report renderer](../src/research_loop/render.py).

### 4. Stop for a reason the user can inspect

The research phase should stop when all material requirements have enough checked support **or** are explicitly unresolved after reasonable search; when the deadline/cap is reached; or when another search is unlikely to improve a material item enough to justify its cost. The last condition is a proposed heuristic and would need empirical calibration: compare the *new supported facts or repaired gaps* gained per additional search/dive on stored runs, not a model's untested confidence. Record which rule fired. Do not turn an untagged incidental deep-dive claim into coverage (current W03), and record degraded planner fallback (current W02). [Workflow addendum](workflow-audit-addendum-2026-09-28.md), [Open Deep Research completion code](https://github.com/langchain-ai/open_deep_research/blob/main/src/open_deep_research/deep_researcher.py).

### 5. Evaluate changes at the same boundaries

Use the existing frozen cases and held-out protocol. For each candidate change, compare: (a) evidence found and independent source support, (b) report coverage and factual/citation quality, (c) completion/failure, cost and time. Grade stored research, claims and the *same visible reader artifact* to locate where a point disappeared; use blind paired report judgments and human review of decisive claims. Track failed runs in the denominator. Before a default change, fix the study cap argument bypass from W01 and retain the prescribed dry/cheap invariant checks. This is a recommendation for the next study design, not approval to run a paid study. [Scout evaluation rules](evaluation.md), [workflow addendum](workflow-audit-addendum-2026-09-28.md).

## Priority and limits

1. **Fix measurement integrity first.** Align evaluators to the visible report and give sentence-level citation/support checks a stable source identity. Without these, a new progress measure can confidently summarize the wrong object.
2. **Expose truthful intermediate state.** Preserve the plan and per-requirement evidence/uncertainty and show operational progress separately. This needs no new run role.
3. **Test whether targeted extra research pays.** On stored trajectories, measure marginal *supported* coverage per extra search or deep dive. Only then consider a dynamic stop rule or extra revision pass.
4. **Keep external benchmark claims in scope.** STORM's article task, OpenScholar's scientific corpus, Anthropic's internal evaluation, GPT Researcher's 10-task self-report, and RACE/DRB2's reference-based judgments have different denominators. Their numbers should not be placed in one leaderboard for Scout. Use their *measurement ideas* and run controlled Scout comparisons on its own frozen tasks.

This review cannot establish which framework would outperform Scout on the same tasks: no common run, budget, model, source policy, or judge was held fixed. It does establish a practical design direction: a report's quality is an outcome; progress is a set of observable transitions toward that outcome, with uncertainty and failure kept explicit.
